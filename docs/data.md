# Data and scenario design

## Provenance

All weather, occupancy, and electricity prices are generated by [scenarios.py](../src/hvac_benchmark/scenarios.py). They are **surrogate experimental inputs**. They are not downloaded observations, a particular city's historical weather, a real utility tariff, or measured occupancy.

This choice keeps provenance clear and the experiment reproducible without distributing a dataset of uncertain origin. No claim of empirical realism or general savings is based on these inputs. Generated input values appear in each trace CSV and can be reconstructed from the seed and source revision. No external dataset license is required; repository-generated data follows the repository license.

## Seasonal days

Each full experiment covers one hypothetical weekday, 00:00-24:00 in a fixed local-clock model without a calendar or daylight-saving transition, followed by 12 hours of empty-zone recovery.

| Period | Mean C | Daily amplitude C | Seed | Weather-error direction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| winter | 8 | 4 | 101 | colder than forecast |
| spring | 16 | 5 | 202 | colder than forecast |
| summer | 28 | 6 | 303 | hotter than forecast |
| autumn | 13 | 5 | 404 | hotter than forecast |

The base weather is a daily cosine peaking near 15:00 plus a 0.6 C second harmonic. The seed sets a smooth whole-day offset in +/-0.7 C, phase shift in +/-0.35 hours, and electricity price offset in +/-0.008 EUR/kWh. There is no independent step noise.

Electricity price is 0.15 EUR/kWh plus smooth morning and evening peaks (amplitudes 0.08 and 0.14 EUR/kWh, centered at 08:00 and 19:00) and the small daily offset. It remains positive. The tariff is assumed announced and known for the operating horizon. It is deliberately illustrative, not an estimate of a local customer bill. Gas is a constant 0.075 EUR/kWh fuel input.

Nominal occupancy is 24 people from 08:00 up to, but not including, 18:00. The same person count drives both sensible heat and CO2 generation. Recovery occupancy is explicitly zero for every controller, including the shorter smoke cases.

## Seven cases per period

| Case | Difference from nominal |
|---|---|
| nominal | Model, planned disturbances, and realized disturbances match |
| weather_mild | A signed 3 C persistent weather error |
| weather_severe | A signed 6 C persistent weather error |
| occupancy_mild | 30 people arriving at 07:00, departing at 18:00 |
| occupancy_severe | 40 people arriving at 06:00, departing at 18:00 |
| loss_mild | Actual UA is 1.20 times nominal; controller retains nominal UA |
| loss_severe | Actual UA is 1.50 times nominal; controller retains nominal UA |

The weather error ramps up between 08:00 and 10:00, stays at full amplitude until 16:00, and returns to zero by 18:00. It is one coherent multi-hour event. The initial weather forecast is the base seasonal profile and cannot foresee the event.

These seven cases separate mechanisms. Combined simultaneous disturbances are not included in this initial benchmark. Mild and severe are experiment labels, not probabilities or calibrated weather/occupancy quantiles.

## Causal information policy

The forecast function accepts only the announced plan and the current observation. It has no argument for future realized weather, future realized occupancy, or actual plant UA.

At each update, the current outdoor-temperature residual relative to the plan is added to the future plan with a four-hour exponential decay. This simple persistence rule cannot predict the onset, exact duration, or reversal of the weather event. It deliberately retains forecast error.

Observed current occupancy replaces the current prediction. If people are already present, that observed count is projected until the known nominal departure time of 18:00. An early arrival is not predicted before it is observed. When nobody is present, future occupancy still follows the announced schedule.

All controllers have perfect current T, CO2, outdoor-temperature, and occupancy observations. Open-loop optimization uses the observation at the start only and then freezes its plan. Later current conditions can affect shared actuator protection, but never replan its schedule. Prices are known by assumption and are not taken from a hidden future-truth channel.

The nominal case is an explicit matched-model reference. There is no separate perfect-information controller in the implementation.

## Evaluation versus development

The full evaluation matrix is fixed: four periods times seven cases times three controllers. There is no train/test fitting or learned parameter. Penalties, forecast-update constants, and baseline rules are fixed engineering choices, not selected to make MPC win.

A development period with seed 11 is defined but excluded from both evaluation suites, reserving a separate input for future tuning. It is not evidence that a tuning study has been performed. If parameters are tuned later, use that development data and preserve the four evaluation seeds.

The smoke suite uses only winter/summer and nominal/severe occupancy over 06:00-10:00, plus recovery. It exercises packaging, solver control, replay, CSV output, and plotting. In this short window early occupants can already be observed at initialization, so it must not be interpreted as the full day-ahead surprise experiment.

## Reproducibility and comparisons

`requirements.txt` pins the reference numerical and plotting libraries; CI uses Python 3.11. Metadata records versions, source commit, seeds, model, solver time budget, and horizon. Time-limited MIP incumbents and elapsed solve time can differ across machines or runs even when inputs are deterministic. This is recorded rather than hidden.

Use the exact same configuration for a paired comparison. Read cost together with temperature/CO2 violation area, duration, and peak, plus terminal recovery and solver fallback use. The plots show scenario-paired changes relative to the thermostat. They do not imply that pooling four constructed days estimates a population average or a statistical confidence interval.

To reproduce the CI full benchmark:

```bash
hvac-benchmark --suite full --time-limit 0.5 --horizon-hours 6 --output results
```

To examine sensitivity, write each setting to a separate directory and compare its metadata. A longer time limit or horizon is a new experiment and should be labeled as such.
